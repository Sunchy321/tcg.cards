# The Magic localization authority is the projected text of record, and display localizations derive from it

For every Magic card, `magic_data.card_localization_authorities` holds the localized text of record — one row per (card, version, locale, source) — and the display rows in `magic.card_localizations` / `magic.card_part_localizations` are derived from it inside the same projection. A row's `source` names the **role** that row plays, not who typed the characters: `''` is the official standard row (the oracle-aligned text in the official style, supplied for Simplified Chinese by MTGCH and stood in for, in every other language, by the newest print's text), and `mtgch` is the folk substitute row, which exists only when no official standard row can be formed. A locale therefore never carries two rows, and the community Chinese text reaches the site through the official standard row rather than alongside it. A row is adopted whole or not at all, and only a value missing where the card actually has one disqualifies it: machine-translated text is adopted, and a vanilla card's absent rules text does not cost it its translated name.

**Why:** The two layers used to be independent — the display layer read official print surfaces while the authority table recorded a separate decision that nothing consumed — which is exactly why they disagreed on 19,132 Simplified Chinese cards while 11,062 more cards had no Chinese row at all. Deriving the display layer from the authority makes the adopted text the text a reader sees, and it is what gives community Chinese an outlet to the site. The provenance triple (`sourceSet`/`sourceNumber`/`sourceReleaseDate`) survives only for rows whose text really is print-established, because a row that uses MTGCH is overwritten by MTGCH no matter which print it anchors to.

**Considered options:** merging the two layers into one table (rejected: the authority stays out of the published fact-table set, and the display rows keep the print join that the site, search and the console editor are built on), deriving the display layer in a view instead of materialising it (rejected for the same publish-boundary reason), and keeping both layers independent with a read-time precedence rule (rejected: it preserves the divergence rather than resolving it).
